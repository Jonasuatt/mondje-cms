Mon Djê in your country

Mon Djê is published from Côte d’Ivoire and can be used in 19 countries whose currency is a franc. Here is what changes from one country to another, and what remains your responsibility. Last updated: 4 November 2026.

In short. Mon Djê is a tracking tool: the till, the stock, the shop’s notebook. It does not replace an accountant, an official filing, or legal or tax advice. The rates, taxes and contributions of your country are the ones you write: outside Côte d’Ivoire, Mon Djê does not know them and does not check them.

1. Countries and currencies

Côte d’Ivoire, Senegal, Mali, Burkina Faso, Benin, Togo, Niger, Guinea-Bissau (West African CFA franc); Cameroon, Gabon, Congo, Chad, Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea (Central African CFA franc); Guinea (Guinean franc), Rwanda (Rwandan franc), Burundi (Burundian franc), Djibouti (Djiboutian franc), Comoros (Comorian franc). Amounts are whole numbers, without cents. Currencies with cents (cedi, naira, dirham, euro, dollar…) are not supported yet.

2. What your country sets in the app

  • The currency shown on prices, invoices and reports.
  • Phone numbers: the dialling code and the expected length (to open WhatsApp). A number written with “+” or “00” and the dialling code always works.
  • Business time: sales stay on your country’s day, even if the owner travels with their phone.
  • The mobile money methods offered: the operators present in your country, and an “other mobile money” method named after the local operator (Airtel Money, D-Money, Lumicash, Free Money, T-Money…). This list is a display aid: it does not guarantee that an operator is available in your town.
  • The language: French or English, on the sign-in screen or in Profile.

3. What you set yourself

  • VAT. You write your country’s rate; Mon Djê only suggests 18% in Côte d’Ivoire. Invoices follow the rate you wrote. With no rate, no VAT line is added.
  • Payroll. The amounts of social contributions and salary tax are the ones the employer enters, under their sole responsibility. Mon Djê neither calculates nor checks them. A Mon Djê payslip proves what was paid; it does not replace a payslip declared to your country’s social security body.
  • Local taxes (tourist tax, electricity for metered homes…): the amount is the one you write.
  • Your tax and social filings remain yours. Mon Djê documents (invoices, receipts, reports, statements) certify what you recorded in the app.

4. Emergency numbers

Côte d’Ivoire has a verified list. In other countries, Mon Djê shows no emergency number at random: a wrong number can cost a life. Staff add the police, fire brigade and ambulance of their own country themselves; they stay on the phone. We will add each country’s list once someone who lives there has confirmed it.

5. Money and mobile money

Mon Djê never receives, holds or transfers its users’ money or their customers’ money. It is not a bank, not a payment institution and not a mobile money operator: it is a notebook. Payments are made between the customer and the shop, through the operator of their choice.

6. The subscription

Outside the West African CFA franc, the subscription is free as long as no payment method is open in your country. The app says so. Nothing is charged: when a payment method opens, you will be told in the app before any payment.

7. Your data outside Côte d’Ivoire

The data of all shops, whatever their country, is hosted in Paris (France). The rights your country’s law gives you over your data apply in addition to those described in the privacy policy (in French): write to contact@mondje.ci.

8. Governing law

The site and the app are governed by Ivorian law; the competent courts are those of Abidjan (see the legal notice). This does not deprive you of the mandatory protections that your country’s law grants you.

9. Languages

The French version prevails. The app and these pages are also offered in English; the English translation of the app was prepared with the help of an artificial intelligence tool and is being reviewed by the team. If two versions differ, the French version applies.
